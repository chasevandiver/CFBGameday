What animation adds

One moment — Ohio State 21 → 28 against Michigan, Q3 8:42, your over 44.5 riding on it — rendered several ways. Hit Replay on any panel, then turn Motion off at the bottom and replay the same thing. The difference between those two viewings is the entire value of the animation, and the panels say what each one costs.

01

A score changes

Seven points. The most common meaningful event on the screen.

no animation 21–24

What it buys: the flash and the flap both make a change you were not looking at findable a second later. Static does not — if you glance away, the event is gone.
What it costs: the flap is slower to read than the flash. That is the real trade: unmistakable from across a room versus instant but easy to miss.
The tween is disqualified, not merely expensive. Watch the trace beside it — it renders 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, none of which were ever the score. Football scores jump by 3, 6, 7 and 8. A tween lies about the thing this product exists to report.
Dependency: split-flap zero · score-pop zero, already shipped · CountUp needs motion.

02

A line moves

Georgia at Alabama, −3 → −1.5. The thing you most want to notice without staring at it, and the thing most likely to happen while the phone is face down on the sofa.

Static −3
Flash +
arrow −3
Split-flap

What it buys: here the flap wins clearly. A line move is a small number changing by a small amount; a flash draws the eye but does not tell you the direction, and the flap shows the digit physically travelling.
What it costs: nothing extra — the mechanism is already built for the score.
Dependency: zero. Note the sign is printed, not flapped — a minus sits exactly on the fold line and is bisected into invisibility otherwise. That bug is live in SplitFlapText as shipped.

03

A game kicks off

Slate B's promotion: a strip at the bottom of the wall grows into the big screen. This is the one animation on this page that is itself a layout change.

Ohio St at MichiganQ3 8:42 · your over 44.5
UGA at Bama7:30P · EDGE

SJSU at Nevada9:30P
PSU at OUPPD

What it buys: you notice a game starting without reading anything. With motion off, the tiles simply jump — the layout is still correct, but the event is invisible unless you happened to be looking.
What it costs: it moves everything below it. Mitigation, and the thing to judge: it fires only on a status change, never on a score update, and never while you have scrolled past the wall.
Dependency: zero — document.startViewTransition() plus view-transition-name is a native drop-in for exactly what MagicBento uses gsap for. Where unsupported it degrades to an instant re-lay-out.

04

Entering the beam

Slate C's mechanism, isolated. Left: one card lit at a time. Right of the toggle: every card lit at once, which is what the slate does today.

Oklahoma at Texas24–34

What it buys: hierarchy without sorting. One card is obviously the subject, and the rest stay readable — which is how you get "the most important number is the largest thing on screen" without reordering anything.
What it costs: if the lit card is not the one you care about, the design is actively working against you. With two live games at once — as today — that is a real scenario, not a corner case.
Dependency: zero. animation-timeline: view() where supported, one IntersectionObserver where not.

06

When the user asked for no motion

globals.css has a global prefers-reduced-motion block that clamps every CSS animation to 0.01ms. Here is what that rule does and does not reach. Emulated below, so you can see it without changing your phone settings — then change them and reload to confirm.

CSS keyframes — score-pop, aura-drift, the flap stopped ✓
CSS transitions — the beam's cross-fade stopped ✓
SVG <animate> — how ElectricBorder ships still moving ✗
Canvas / rAF — how ClickSpark and GooeyNav ship still moving ✗

This is the panel that matters most for the original question. Three of the zero-dependency React Bits components — ElectricBorder, ClickSpark, GooeyNav — animate outside CSS, so the app's global kill switch does not touch them. Adopting any of them means hand-writing a matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)") guard that the component does not ship with.
SplitFlapText is the exception: it handles reduced motion itself. That is a real reason to prefer it.
