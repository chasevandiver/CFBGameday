Design directions

Two rounds of exploration mode, per docs/DESIGN.md. Open them on a phone, in a dim room, at arm's length — that is the environment they are for. Nothing here is app code and nothing here ships.

Slate — React Bits round

Three directions for /slate, each organised around a different React Bits technique reimplemented in vanilla CSS/JS — which is how the real adoption cost becomes visible before anything is installed. Same seven games in all three, from the app's own fixture set, plus Miami (OH) at Western Michigan as the string-length test. Two games are live at once on purpose.

  1. Start here

    What animation adds

    Not a direction — the question itself. One moment (Ohio State 21 → 28) rendered several ways, with a Replay button on every panel and a global motion off switch, so you can watch the same event with and without. Includes a live FPS readout on 70 drifting auras, and a panel showing exactly which techniques ignore the reduced-motion setting.

    under test: CountUp vs SplitFlapText vs what ships today

  2. Slate A

    Departure Board

    The whole Saturday as one mechanical board. Every game is a 44px fixed-width row, rows never reorder, and the only thing that moves is a character flipping inside its own cell. "What's new" lives in a CHANGED strip so it never has to sort the board.

    density high · leads with what changed · live = the digit flaps · CTA a slip rail in the board

    tests SplitFlapText · zero deps · the only shortlisted component that already handles reduced motion

  3. Slate B

    The Wall

    A sports bar's wall of TVs. Tile area is proportional to how much the game matters to you, and a game that kicks off physically grows into a bigger screen. The most literal reading of "the most important number is the largest thing on screen" — and the direction most at odds with "no layout shift."

    density variable · leads with the shape of the day · live = the tile promotes · CTA inline in the big tile

    tests MagicBento (needs gsap) → replaced by startViewTransition() · zero deps

  4. Slate C

    The Light

    A dark room where only the card nearest the middle of the screen is lit. The lit card gets the 64px score and the only tappable odds cells on the page — so the primary action is wherever the light is, and there is no hero and no floating button at all.

    density medium-low · leads with what's under your thumb · live = light · CTA the lit card is the button

    tests SpotlightCard, ElectricBorder, GradualBlur · all zero deps · but ElectricBorder needs a hand-written reduced-motion guard

Home hub — earlier round

Three directions for /. Direction A is what shipped. These predate the naming scheme; they would be hub-a/b/c.html if we ever touch them.

  1. Direction A

    In the language

    Nothing invented — every value already exists in the app's stylesheet. Fixes the structure instead: two sections, a wider column that splits in two on a tablet, the slate's team-colour aura lit on your rows, a width-capped CTA, and a held-vs-now column.

    density medium · leads with how many games you're on · live = chips · CTA in the hero

  2. Direction B

    The board

    A sportsbook's wall. Every position is one row on a shared grid, so you compare down a column instead of reading cards. Remembered by the move column: your number against the current number, coloured.

    density high · leads with the numbers · live = pulsing row edge · CTA sticky at the bottom

  3. Direction C

    Broadcast

    A score bug. The next game you have action on takes the whole top of the screen in its teams' colours; when it kicks, the card floods with the verdict. Everything else compresses to tiles.

    density low · leads with one game · live = the card floods · CTA a floating pill

What was deliberately not tested, and why

  • Aurora, Particles, Lightning, Ribbons (need ogl) — animated WebGL on a screen kept open four hours next to a TV. Battery and thermals; the aura-drift measurement in globals.css already says how this ends. See panel 05 of the motion study.
  • ScrollStack (needs lenis) — smooth-scroll hijacking, a direct violation of "never steal scroll position." The worst fit in the catalog for this product.
  • ClickSpark, Magnet, GlareHover, TiltedCard — pointer-driven. There is no cursor on the only device this product targets.
  • Dock, Stepper, Carousel, Stack (need motion) — wrong screen. Possibly relevant to the bet slip; not to the slate.
  • GradualBlur — cut as a direction. Its mechanism is 6–10 stacked backdrop-filter layers, and globals.css:342-345 already argues the opposite case. The real problem it targets is fixed by mask-image: linear-gradient(...) on the scroll container — one line, zero components. Used that way in Slate A's filter bar.
