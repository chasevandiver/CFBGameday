Direction D — The Sheet

The hero rule, stated once: the top bet gets the panel iff exactly one bet sits alone at the highest tier present. That single sentence produces all four states above — hero, no hero on a tie, no hero when everything is one tier, and hero for a lone bet. It lives in share-card.ts as heroBet(), a pure function the layout reads and nothing else, so the rule is testable on its own rather than buried in JSX.

Headings print on tier change, not per row. Compare states 1 and 3: seven bets each, but state 3 needs one heading instead of six. That is what buys back A's vertical cost while keeping A's words — “Bet of the Afternoon Slate” prints in full, with the broadcast window interpolated from the bet's own kickoff via the existing kickSlot(). B could only ever have said SLATE.

The right-hand column is the reason this is an image. Fixed width, tabular figures, Plex Mono — 3.0u over 2.0u over 1.5u, decimal under decimal. share-text.ts opens by conceding that the text share cannot do this.

Both hard cases are in every state: Miami (OH) at Western Michigan as the longest matchup string the product can produce, and a futures bet with no game — monogram instead of logos, an em-dash where the clock would be, so the column keeps its shape.

Sort order is visible in state 3, where every tier is equal and the rows fall in pure kickoff order: 11:00, 2:30, 3:30, 6:00, 7:00, 9:30, then the futures bet with no kickoff last. In states 1 and 2 the same rule runs inside each tier.
